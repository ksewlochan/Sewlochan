# The Power of Lists
---

80% of Project Management comes down to one simple thing
- Managing lists

It is that simple, and that complex

So, what is a list?

- Table of Contents
{:toc}

Last updated: {{ page.last_updated_at }}

# The Anatomy of a List

List Item
- First: Start at the Bottom – The List Item
- Contains the details of the item
- Could be assigned to someone
- Could have due dates
- Could have labels
- Could have lots of things

List Group
- Second: Next Group the List Items
- By label
- By assignee
- By due date
- By whatever makes sense
- Items can be in multiple groups

List
- Third: You Have a Set of Groups That Form a List
- Represents a project
- Or a high-level task
- Or some thing that needs to be accomplished

# Why Do We Want Lists

Tasks can be complex.

But a large task can be broken down into a few smaller sub-tasks.
- And so-on to smaller sub-sub-tasks
- Etc.

At the end you have small tasks that are easy to accomplish but need to be organized into a larger whole

# Some Example Lists

Grocery Shopping
- Small
- Could just be you or include your family
- Simple labels for “need” and “bought”

Holiday Shopping
- More complex
- More labels
- Maybe assignees as people purchase items for you

Business Project
- Yet more complex
- Many tasks
- Many groups
- Many assignees
- Many  labels

Enterprise Change Management
- May be even more complex
- Across an organization

All are a collection of tasks that must be organized across several people (possibly one) accomplishing small items that feed up to a larger accomplishment.


## Personal Lists

Requirements
- Just for you or a few close family or friends
- Simple or no labelling
- Simple grouping
- Simple done criteria

Options
- Google Keep
- Microsoft To Do
- Microsoft OneNote
- Evernote

## Business Lists

Requirements
- Shared among colleagues
- More complex labelling

Options
- Atlassian JIRA tasks
- Atlassian Confluence pages
- Shared Microsoft excel or word files

Need More Complexity

Requirements
- Need more organization?

Options
- Trello
- ZenHub
- GitHub Projects
- Jira

# Key Takeaways

The tool does not matter
- Pick the right tool that will accomplish the task at hand

The Philosophy Matters
- Complex tasks can be broken down into simpler sub-tasks
- Simple subtasks can be organized into a variety of groups
- As simple subtasks are completed the larger, complex tasks are achieved
